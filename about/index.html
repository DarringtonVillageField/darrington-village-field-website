The Village Field Trust

Caring for the Field, together

The registered charity responsible for protecting, maintaining and improving Darrington Village Field for the benefit of the community.

Our purpose

A field held for public benefit

Darrington Village Field is cared for through Darrington Village Field Trust — not by Wakefield Council, the Parish Council or by the Church.

The Trust’s purpose is to provide and maintain the playing field and play equipment for public use in Darrington. It works to keep the space welcoming today while protecting it for future generations.

How the Trust works

A great deal happens behind the scenes

Trustees carry the charity’s formal responsibility, but caring for the Field and delivering its events is a shared effort involving a fantastic committee, wonderful local people, trusted contractors, community organisations and businesses.

01

Year-round care

Regular grass cutting, twice-yearly hedge cutting, litter picking, weeding and general inspections keep the Field welcoming. Trustees undertake some work, contractors handle specialist maintenance and local people help with jobs such as benches, gates and entrances.

02

Equipment safety

The Trustees conduct monthly playground and exercise-equipment checks, looking for damage, failures, loose or missing parts and vandalism. Issues are followed up with specialist suppliers, whilst an independent formal inspection is carried out once a year.

03

Responsible management

Behind the scenes, the Trustees perform all the governance: creating and updating policies, managing accounts and finances, ensuring adequate insurance is in place, maintaining licence compliance, conducting risk assessments, managing suppliers, evaluating and prioritising projects, event planning and the Trust’s reporting responsibilities to the Charity Commission.

04

Events that sustain the Field

The income needed to maintain the Village Field is generated by the Feast & Fayre, Five Mile Run and Bonfire Night. These community events ensure that routine maintenance is carried out, whilst larger projects can be planned and costed.

The work behind event day

Months of preparation create a few memorable hours

Each event depends on people giving time steadily in the weeks and months beforehand, followed by an intensive effort to set up, run and clear away safely.

Feast & FayreA core team of over 16 people spends over five months and hundreds of hours planning and preparing, joined over the weekend by more than 20 volunteers and helpers.
Bonfire NightThe core team spends up to four months preparing the event, with another 15 or more people assisting over the weekend.
Five Mile RunThough part of Feast & Fayre, a small team of three people focuses on organising the Run, with another six people joining them on the day.
Santa SpecialA smaller, dedicated team of 10 people works with Santa over a few weeks so he can greet the children of the village. This is a great event for involving younger volunteers who join in with the festive spirit.

Investment people can see

Recent projects have included removing a large diseased tree, installing a path along one side of the Field, fitting new gates and gateposts, and investing £75,000 in new play equipment.

Event equipment also needs periodic investment, from cooking equipment and awnings to signs and other practical essentials.

The next improvements

Potential projects include new picnic tables, paths along two more sides of the Field and a new surface at the main entrance.

The Trust is moving from reacting to immediate needs towards identifying priorities, formalising event and financial management, and agreeing how the Field should evolve over the next three to five years.

Working together

A village-wide effort

The Trust’s work reaches well beyond the trustees. Different parts of village life contribute equipment, skills, performances, communication and practical support.

Church Farm

Farm representatives contribute through the committee, provide tractors and vehicles, supply straw-bale seating and organise much of the produce sold at Feast & Fayre.

Darrington School

The school takes part in Feast & Fayre with its own stall and a traditional Maypole-dancing performance.

Parish Council

Darrington Parish Council attends Feast & Fayre and helps publicise Village Field activities through its newsletter.

Local trades

Darrington Village is very lucky to have a whole host of people with skills who contribute to the maintenance and improvement of the Village Field.

Our story

Rooted in village history

Darrington Village Field was acquired for the community in the 1970s, with Darrington Village Field Trust registered as a charity on 13 January 1977.

That same year was Queen Elizabeth II’s Silver Jubilee and, in celebration, the community came together to hold a fayre on the Field, with people from the village dressed in Edwardian costumes. The event helped inspire what became the Feast & Fayre, with the Edwardian Lady remaining a symbol of what community spirit can create.

We continue to grow memories of the Field’s history, so if you have any photographs, documents or memories that you can share, feel free to contact the Trust and we can look to add them to our heritage.
